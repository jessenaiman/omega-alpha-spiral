# Floor One Design Brief

## Milestone

Floor One is a standalone, one-floor modern existential NetHack/Rogue demo. It gives the player a basic taste of narrative exploration, action-roguelite pressure, and psychological action-adventure. Later narrative scenes and later floors are separate milestones.

The slice proves one representative encounter before adding content breadth.

## Player Promise

Enter an uncertain remembered space, read what it wants from you, and choose whether to speak, strike, or flee before the floor closes around you.

## Target Feeling

The player should feel cautious, implicated, and increasingly capable of reading consequences. The world is threatening but legible: it waits while the player thinks, then answers every costly action.

The three Dreamweavers approach problems differently, but no verb permanently belongs to one Dreamweaver. Talk, Hit, and Run remain valid player verbs regardless of perspective or affinity.

## Play Pattern

The primary activity is exploring a discrete grid under limited visibility. Talk, Hit, and Run are the initial problem-solving verbs.

Every 5-30 seconds, the player reveals terrain, reads the short message history, evaluates the Threshold Guard or route, and commits to movement, waiting, conversation, combat, or retreat. Information actions do not spend a turn.

Across 1-5 minutes, unknown space becomes remembered terrain, the Threshold Guard's disposition and health may change, a useful pickup changes the player's options, pursuit raises pressure, and the route to the exit becomes understandable.

## Reward, Risk, and Learning

Reward comes from gaining reliable information, changing access through Talk, surviving or resolving the Threshold Guard, collecting the useful pickup, and reaching the exit stairs. Risk comes from spending turns near a pursuing enemy while finite HP limits mistakes. This milestone does not add hunger.

A better player distinguishes free information from turn-costing actions, predicts the monster phase, uses terrain and doors, chooses when Talk is safer than Hit, and uses Run for committed travel or tactical retreat without overshooting danger.

Death ends the attempt. The message history explains the decisive outcomes, and immediate retry reconstructs the same seed so the player can apply what was learned. A new run may select a new seed. Reaching the exit ends the demo and signals the next visual era rather than loading another floor.

## Communication

The grid, visibility treatment, actor posture, door state, HP display, and short message history communicate the next decision. Unknown space reveals nothing. Remembered terrain remains readable but does not preserve entity positions. Currently visible entities are shown only while in sight.

## Controls

Desktop keyboard is first. Cardinal movement uses arrow keys or WASD. Optional diagonal movement may use eight-direction keys. Wait spends one turn. Talk and explicit Hit target an adjacent actor; bumping a hostile actor may also perform melee. Run commits repeated movement in a direction until an obstacle, threat, or point of interest stops it. Inspect, help, and other information-only actions cost no turn.

Mobile receives a layout review only in this milestone; touch play is not an acceptance requirement.

## Non-Goals

- Multiple floors or later narrative scenes
- A procedural floor generator
- A broad inventory or equipment system
- A deep dialogue tree
- A save system
- Hunger or other unapproved survival meters
- Final balance or production-scale content variety
- Rapier, continuous physics, or physics-driven collision
